MINALUNGAO NATIONAL PARK

Minalungao National Park is a protected area in General Tinio, Nueva Ecija, Philippines, known for its limestone formations and the Peñaranda River. It's promoted as an ecotourism destination with activities like raft riding, cliff diving, swimming, and hiking to the 1000-step hill to see a large glass cross. The park features up to 16-meter high limestone walls, unique rock formations, and a system of subterranean caverns.

Grabe, ang ganda ng Minalungao Park! Hindi ko inasahan na ganito ka-presko at ka-relaxing. Pagdating pa lang namin, ramdam ko na agad ‘yung malamig na simoy ng hangin at ‘yung tunog ng ilog na parang musika. Naglakad kami sa mga batong puti na parang marmol, tapos sumakay kami sa bamboo raft para mas mapuntahan ‘yung gitna ng ilog. Ang linaw ng tubig—kitang-kita mo ‘yung mga bato sa ilalim! Sa tanghali, nag-picnic kami sa lilim ng mga puno at bumili ng inihaw na tilapia at banana cue sa mga tindahan doon. Simple lang pero sobrang sulit. Sana lang mas ayusin pa ‘yung daan papasok, pero overall, sulit na sulit ang biyahe. Perfect para sa barkadahan o kahit solo trip kung gusto mong mag-recharge.

Norbert

Planning a day at Minalungao Park? Here’s an ideal itinerary I invented: arrive early (8:00 AM) to beat midday heat; walk the main trail to the first viewpoint, then take the riverside path to the boat landing for a 45–60 minute raft or kayak trip. Pack a lunch and plenty of water, wear sturdy sandals that can get wet, and bring a light rain layer just in case. There are small vendors near the entrance selling snacks and cold drinks, and restrooms near the main picnic area are basic but clean. Don’t forget a power bank if you’re shooting a lot of photos. If you want a quieter visit, late afternoon is magical, but plan to leave before dusk for safety. Overall: scenic cliffs, good shallow river spots, friendly local guides, and enough variety to fill either a relaxed half-day or a full-day outing.

Angles

We treated ourselves to a spontaneous overnight near Minalungao Park and it might be the most romantic ‘no-plan’ getaway I’ve ever had. The trail to the river is easy enough for slow strolls, which made for plenty of time to hold hands and point out tiny discoveries — an orchid tucked into a limestone crack, a school of minnows darting in the shallows. We found a little cove where the current calmed and there was space to lay out a blanket. As the sun set, the cliffs glowed warm and the sound of crickets rose up like a soft chorus. Staff were friendly and helped us arrange a small bonfire area (permitted), and the local cottage where we stayed the night served the best grilled tilapia I’ve tasted — simple, smoky, and just-right after a day outdoors. Perfect spot if you want scenic beauty with a cozy, unplugged vibe.

Kenneth Angles

I spent an entire day at Minalungao Park and left with my camera full of shots and my heart full of calm. We hiked a winding trail that opened suddenly onto a river framed by sheer limestone cliffs — the kind of view that makes you forget your phone battery exists. We rented a small bamboo raft and drifted beneath the cliffs while the guide pointed out native birds nesting in crevices. Midday we found a shaded rock ledge and unpacked a picnic of warm pan de sal and fresh mangoes; the river’s gentle murmur felt like background music. In the late afternoon the light softened and the water turned a deep, glassy green — perfect reflection time. Facilities were basic but well-kept, and the few local vendors sold excellent homemade snacks. Highly recommended for anyone who wants an active day without the crowds or for writers looking for a quiet place to think.

Seline Bernardo
